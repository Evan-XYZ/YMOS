# Inconsistency patterns

Use these patterns to ask better questions. Do not diagnose from a label alone.

## 1. Judge switching

Entry relies on one judge; losses are defended using another. Ask which judge had invalidation authority before entry.

## 2. Horizon drift

A time-bounded idea loses its deadline after the expected event fails. Ask what evidence justified extending the horizon.

## 3. Size substitutes for conviction

The user increases size to feel certain or recover losses. Ask whether the evidence improved independently of cost basis.

## 4. Price and thesis are conflated

Price movement is treated as both proof and disproof without a stated relationship to the thesis. Ask when price is evidence and when it is noise.

## 5. Self-label conflicts with behavior

The user names one family but repeatedly enters, exits, or monitors like another. Preserve both descriptions and ask for priority.

## 6. Cadence mismatch

The stated horizon is long, but high-frequency inputs repeatedly create new decisions. Ask which decisions genuinely require that frequency.

## 7. Hidden shared exposure

Several positions are counted as diversified although they depend on the same sector, factor, event, currency, or liquidity condition.

## 8. Hindsight overwrite

The original thesis is rewritten after the outcome. Ask for the earliest timestamped record and separate original from later interpretation.

## 9. Outcome-only learning

A profitable rule violation is praised or a disciplined loss is condemned. Audit process and evidence separately from outcome.

## 10. Data-quality masquerades as judgment

An apparent strategy failure began with stale, missing, or misclassified evidence. Fix the driver before rewriting the kernel.

## 11. Gate proliferation

Every mistake adds another checklist item until the workflow becomes unusable. Locate the earliest decisive interception point instead of adding gates everywhere.

## 12. Rule relaxation after pain

A loss immediately produces a looser rule. Require a change proposal with counter-evidence, review sample, and rollback before adoption.
